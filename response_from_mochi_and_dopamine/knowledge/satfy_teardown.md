The two channels post about the same amount. The gap is how much each video pulls, not volume.

## What they do
1. **They answer questions people already have,** using famous things: π, infinity, a googol, Fibonacci, 0.999…=1, buses to the Moon. Our titles set up made-up contests ("Fast vs Careful: whose box tower…") that nobody was wondering about.
2. **They franchise hard, making the number bigger each episode:**
   - **Counting** (8 episodes): a million 32K → a billion 92K → a trillion 100K → a googol 56K → a quintillion 31K → a googolplex 63K → infinity 49K.
   - **π from collisions** (7 episodes): 300K, 127K, 58K, 32K, 32K, 10K, 7.7K.
   - **Chocolate paradox:** 48K, then 17K.
   - **"16 levels of X":** 5 episodes.
3. **Each long video extends their best Short series.** Counting led to 151K in 7 days. The π blocks became a long one at 15K in 3 days, and paper folding is the newest. Long videos built from new ideas flopped (art equations 776, beats 665, stickman 1.7K).
4. **They make sequels viewers asked for:** "You asked for the Sun: 16 digits of π" got 39K.
5. **They drop flops fast.** Their first 10 Shorts got ~2K each (where we are now). The love/beats/motivation experiments went under 4K and were dropped.
6. **Their titles are short,** 4–9 words with one emoji. The Shorts tab shows no hashtags in them.
7. **No characters.** One clear theme ("maths wonders") gives people a reason to subscribe.

## What we did wrong
- **The "never duplicate" rule turned off franchising.** The press series has only 4 episodes despite 281K + 143K, and every other video is a new mechanic.
- **Long video late.** SatFy's long videos started ~3 weeks ago. Ours is still in review.
- **Two channels and many mechanics** spread our effort thin, and neither channel has one clear theme.
- **Our questions are made up, not ones people already ask.**
