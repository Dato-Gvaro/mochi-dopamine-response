# What stops a doom-scroller: research → rules (2026-10-13)

The owner's goal: "brainrot" Shorts that hook an aimless scroller in the first seconds and keep them watching. The evidence below is mixed. Lab attention research is solid on motion/looming/luminance; colour and sound evidence is weaker (mostly practitioner sources). Our own data and the reference channel fill the gaps.

## Evidence
- **Motion onset captures attention automatically:** a change from stillness to movement grabs the eye (Abrams & Christ 2003; PMC review).
- **LOOMING (an object growing / approaching on a collision course) captures attention strongest**, especially from the periphery; near-misses don't (Lin et al., Current Biology 2009; Sugarman et al. 2021).
- **Abrupt LUMINANCE changes pop out; abrupt colour changes alone don't** (Theeuwes 1995). Brightness difference matters more than hue (Applied Sciences 2024).
- **Faces with clear emotion are read instantly** (practitioner consensus).
- **Curiosity gap:** an incomplete/in-progress scene ("what happens next?") holds attention. A pattern interrupt (something unexpected) triggers the orienting response (practitioner sources).
- **Colour comfort:** moderate saturation with good brightness, and blue/green/purple combinations, read as calming and are comfortable for sustained watching. Pastels evoke positive emotion. Very saturated neon grabs attention but can fatigue (eye-tracking studies 2023-24; Frontiers 2024).
- **Pacing has a ceiling:** fast-paced short video produces fragmented, reactive attention (eye-tracking 2026); more cuts help only up to a point (2026 marketing study).
- **"Oddly satisfying" = order + completion + predictability** ("just right" feeling; mirror-neuron explanation); viewers use it to relax (Discover, Live Science, Wikipedia).
- **Sound:** sound effects synced to visual events (pops, whooshes, risers before a reveal) hold attention and signal "something is about to happen" (practitioner sources). Satisfying = crisp, material-true, predictable sounds; no harshness.
- **Ours:** the brightest openings with big characters and a one-glance goal held 48-51%; dark, small, busy ones 24-31%. **Reference channel:** one rule, one big counter, one object under a threat already moving at frame 0, neon on black; median 34K views.
- **Owner:** dislikes reverse/rewind openings ("doesn't look nice"), slow starts, long slow-motion endings, and documentary pacing.

## RULES for every clip (from 2026-10-13)
1. **Frame 0 is mid-action, never a rewind.** Something is ALREADY moving (motion onset) and something is coming TOWARD the viewer or toward a target (looming/collision course: a press descending, a boulder flying at the wall, a ball about to hit). The first satisfying event lands **by 1.0 s**, the rule is clear **by 2 s**, the stakes by 3 s.
2. **One clear focal point at the start** (one ball, one machine, one face), big and bright against a dark background. Density grows from there; never open on clutter.
3. **Luminance contrast over hue:** a bright subject on a dark stage. A **2-3 colour palette**: a warm accent (coral/orange/gold) vs a cool accent (teal/cyan/mint), plus violet/blue for calm structure. Medium-high saturation, never muddy, never a rainbow mess. Mochi = warm coral, Bolt = cool mint (already the complementary pair).
4. **Faces:** Mochi/Bolt visible with a clear expression in the first second (not tiny dots). React on key events.
5. **Event rhythm:** a satisfying event every ~0.3-0.6 s from the first second, accelerating; a pattern break (new rule step, lead change, twist) every 3-5 s. No dead air, no long holds.
6. **Satisfying = order + completion:** things snapping into place, filling, splitting cleanly, a final "complete" state.
7. **Sound from frame 0, every visible event has a crisp, material-true sound** (wood = tok, stone = crack/crunch, metal = clink, glass = ting, liquid = glug). Risers into the climax; a big satisfying payoff hit. Pleasant, never harsh; vary repeats slightly.
8. **Length 20-35 s** unless the escalation honestly needs more; no slow motion longer than ~1 s at the end; show the full payoff (don't cut before the result lands).
9. **Loop** by a seamless cut/dissolve to the opening state, not by a visible rewind.
