## 2. Do UNUSUAL objects win? (within-channel test)
858 recent uploads from 13 size/scale channels, titles tagged by keyword. Each video's views are divided by **its own channel's median**, which controls for channel size.

| Title type | n | Median × channel | Share ≥ 3× |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Absurd premise / what-if** ("if insects were 1000× bigger", "first person", "in a city", "at human scale") | 63 | **2.10×** | **43%** |
| Space / universe | 102 | 1.38× | 34% (and the biggest outliers: 162M, 50M) |
| Real objects / human-made | 100 | 1.18× | 25% |
| Fictional / monster / meme creatures | 202 | 1.00× | 26% |
| Real animals | 102 | 0.91× | 25% |
| Odd body part (eyes, teeth, skulls, feet) | 81 | 0.84× | 38% (high variance: eyeballs 23.8M) |

**Answer: weird objects alone do not outperform (1.0×). An absurd PREMISE does (2.1×).** "A domino taller than the universe" is that premise. So unusual objects are the **spice** that makes people stop and watch, mixed with familiar **anchors** that let the viewer feel the size: you can't feel "Goliath grouper", but you can feel "taller than a giraffe". Recent weird hits (brainrot, SCP, Backrooms, Poppy Playtime, Attack on Titan, Godzilla) are **copyrighted or kid-coded**, so they are excluded. Caveat: the tags come from crude title keywords. Treat the result as a direction, not a law.

- **Made-for-kids risk.** Emoji, cartoon-face, and toy styles of size comparison ARE the kids' genre: "HUNGRY PLANETS / Planet SIZES for BABY" 88.8M, "Fat, Fatter, Fattest" 293M, KidsLearningTube "Universe Size Comparison" 35M, and a Numberblocks-style "WON'T STOP Growing?!" (7.7M, 2026-03). YouTube's own guidance says animation and bright colours alone don't make a video "for kids"; characters, toys and kid themes do. **Rules for us:** no faces or expressions on objects or planets (animal eyes as small dots only), no toys, candy or cartoon characters, no kid tunes. Use adult hooks: money stacks, records, creepy deep sea, black holes, "every human stacked".
- **Brands and people.** Use generic labels: TALLEST BUILDING (not the building's name), JUMBO JET, CRUISE SHIP, A CAR. Record holders appear without names (tallest man, highest skydive). Money stacks are stylised green bricks, never a realistic note face (US currency illustration rules).
- **Effort, honestly.** 182 objects at the stills' level of detail (5-15 primitives each, see `ol_domino_style_test.py`) break down into about 35 hero objects (more detail), about 90 standard ones, and about 57 space objects from 8 parametric generators (planet disc with bands/craters, star with glow, nebula cloud, galaxy spiral, cluster dots, black-hole ring, orbit ellipse, cosmic web). The space third is cheap. The Earth-scale ~125 objects are the real work, roughly 2-3 builder sessions.

## 4. Recommendation
**A. Flat vector poster (recommended)** (`style_A_flat_vector.png`)
- **Objects:** code-drawn flat illustrations with 2-tone cel shading (a hard light/shadow split) and no outlines. Kurzgesagt (flat vector; not frame-read here) proves flat vector can carry this genre to 30M+ ("The Largest Star in the Universe" 35.8M, "Largest Black Hole" 33.0M), and it forgives imperfect shapes. Not Kurzgesagt's look: our palette, no faces.
- **Dominoes:** **ivory with black pips** (reads as "domino" like the 66M/117M hits). Colour comes from the objects and the sky (the multi-hue research rule). Flag: this drops the hue-ramp dominoes, so the owner decides.
- **Density:** **182 objects**, 101 of them unusual (55%). About **every other domino in the first 2 minutes** (27 objects/min, 1 per 2.2 s) and every 3-5 dominoes later (16-20/min). Median gap 2.4 s, longest 8.9 s. True sizes thin out in deep space, so that is the honest ceiling without padding.
- **Camera:** about 2.75× (vs 3.3), framed on the front, so the START leaves the frame after about 10 s. The chain and the beaten objects recede left and shrink, and the **next** object waits on the right ("will it beat it?"). Progress stays in the counter and the bottom meter.
- **Labels:** NAME + metric / imperial above each object, newest 6-7 only, gold flash when beaten. The big banner "DOMINO 49 BEATS THE CORPSE FLOWER" only for about 25 hero beats (1 per ~20 s); a banner per object (every 2 s) would be noise.
- **Pace:** keep 1.12 and the current timeline (50 → 80 falls/min, 9:15). The roster times below are on that timeline.
- **Space:** the sky darkens with altitude (brief), and objects there become glowing flat discs/spirals. Option: slide into style B's glow in space, so A turns into B gradually.

**B. Neon line art on black (alternative)** (`style_B_neon_line.png`)
- The parent channel's identity, the cheapest to draw (strokes only), and the strongest in space.
- Weaker object recognition at human scale (outlines only).
- Lowest MFK risk.

**C. Sticker cut-outs (alternative)** (`style_C_sticker_cutout.png`)
- Thick white die-cut border + drop shadow, colourful dominoes with white pips, tilted card labels (Reigarw, 24M).
- The most "wtf, stop and look" energy, and readable on any background.
- **The highest MFK risk** (stickers read playful). Only with adult object choices and a less candy palette.

**Not recommended:** emoji/icon sets (licence + MFK + not authored), photo cut-outs (rule 1, per-file licences), and black silhouettes for everyday objects.

**Where the data disagrees with the owner:**
1. "Silhouettes don't work": they do in this genre (10.2M, and 2.0M in 9 days), for creatures whose OUTLINE is the weird part, shown with a photo reveal. Our test failed on **density** (2 objects in 28 s vs 1 per 2-5 s) and **boring picks**, not only the silhouette.
2. "Compare unusual stuff": yes as spice (55%), but keep about 45% familiar anchors. An all-weird roster loses the feeling of size, and weird subjects average only 1.0× their channel median.
3. "Every other domino": fine for the first 2 minutes. Long-form references run 1 per 3.5-8.5 s, and at 2 s each a viewer can read at most a label per object, so the hero banners must be rarer.
4. Biggest risk is not the style: no long-form growing-domino video has ever broken out. Cut the first 50 s (1.5 cm → a person) as a Short to test the hook cheaply.
